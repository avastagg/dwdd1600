Who Were the Signers

Famous Signers

There were 56 signers of the Declaration of Independence, but some became far more famous than others. The biggest names include:

Thomas Jefferson
Probably the most famous signer. He was the principal author of the Declaration and later became the 3rd U.S. president.
Benjamin Franklin
Scientist, inventor, diplomat, and one of the most recognizable Founding Fathers. He was 70 when he signed.
John Adams
Major advocate for independence, future 2nd president, and later Jefferson's political rival.
John Hancock
President of the Continental Congress and famous for his enormous signature at the bottom. He later became governor of Massachusetts.
Samuel Adams
Revolutionary leader and organizer in Massachusetts; cousin of John Adams.
John Witherspoon
A minister and president of the College of New Jersey (later Princeton); the only active college president to sign.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton
Wealthy Maryland planter and the last surviving signer; he lived until 1832.
Samuel Chase
Maryland politician who later became a U.S. Supreme Court justice.
George Read
Delaware politician who later became Chief Justice of Delaware.
Roger Sherman
Connecticut politician and one of the few people who signed the Declaration, Articles of Confederation, and U.S. Constitution.
Robert Morris
Extremely wealthy Philadelphia merchant who became an important financier of the Revolution.
George Walton
Georgia signer who later served as governor of Georgia.

Signers by State

Although interestingly, only Jefferson, Franklin, and Hancock actually signed. John Adams and Samuel Adams were deeply involved in the independence movement but were not among the 56 signers.
